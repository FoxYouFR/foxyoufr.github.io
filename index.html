Hi, I'm a Master's student in Neuro-X at EPFL, focusing on brain-like artificial models.
I aim to work on three different aspects:

  • Building models of brain disorders and diseases, to allow for quick neuroscientific exploration
  • Linking artificial models with the brain and vice-versa to improve our understanding of both systems
  • Using neuroscience to improve interpretability and safety of artificial models

Projects Showcase

2025 · Master Semester Project | Prof. Martin Schrimpf

NeuroAI Project @ NeuroAI Lab (EPFL)

Modeling Brain Response to Dynamic Visual Stimuli Using Enhanced Neural Networks for Motion Processing: Implemented, engineered and tested multiple SOTA vision models using PyTorch with the goal of obtaining high alignment with fMRI brain activations.

2026 · Master Semester Project | Prof. Adrien Doerig

NeuroAI Project @ Cognitive Computational Neuroscience lab (FUB)

Implied auditory information from natural scenes presented visually: Research whether images of natural scenes can provoke similar responses as auditory stimuli corresponding to those scenes. Preliminary findings suggest that some variance can only be explained by the visual information.

2025 · IC Research Project | Prof. Carmela Troncoso

IC Project @ Spring Lab (EPFL)

Proposed a new benchmark for context-aware moderation using a context-augmented LLM annotator. Showed limitations of current approaches and how our method is a first step towards moderation as an empowering tool for marginalized communities.

2023 · Bachelor Research Project | Prof. Pavan Ramdya

RL Project @ Neuroengineering Laboratory (EPFL)

Social NeuroMechFly 2.0: Improved a Gym Drosophilia simulation for behavioral studies. Upgraded the program to allow for multiple entities in a single simulation and added new capacities for the simulated animals

2023 · Summer In the Lab | Prof. Alexander Mathis

Research Internship @ Alexander Mathis Group of Computational Neuroscience & AI

Studied the conditions for transformer models to keep simulated animal walking even when the attention layer is frozen. Presented the results during a symposium at EPFL.
